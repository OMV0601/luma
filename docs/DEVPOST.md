# FoolProof — get scammed here, so it never happens out there

> ℹ️ **Reference only — this is the written Devpost submission story, not a pitch script.** For Fri Aug 21 use **pitch/FoolProof-Demo-Day-Script.docx** (the two-person run of show). Note it predates The Notario, so it still says five scenarios and 27 tests.

## Inspiration

In 2025, Americans reported **$15.9 billion** lost to fraud — a record, up 27% in a single year — and **$3.5 billion of it came from imposter scams alone** (FTC). What struck us wasn't the size of the number. It was the mismatch between how money is *lost* and how financial literacy is *taught*.

Every tool we found was passive: articles, videos, quizzes. But nobody loses $2,000 to a quiz. Money is taken **in live conversation** — a convincing person, applying pressure, in the moment, before you have time to think. And the scams targeting people our age don't look like "scams" at all: they look like a dream internship DM, a toll-fee text, a first apartment lease.

You don't build a reflex by reading about one. So we built the opposite of a literacy app: **a room full of AI adversaries that try to rob you — safely.**

## What it does

FoolProof is an adversarial training simulator. You enter the Gauntlet and face five playable scams, each a live AI character running a documented manipulation playbook:

- 🤝 **Quick Cash Danny** — a payday lender hiding a triple-digit APR inside "$15 per $100"
- 💼 **The Dream Internship** — the fake-check overpayment job scam hitting students right now
- 📞 **The Fraud Alert** — a bank imposter, in **full voice mode**: spoofed caller ID, a real ringtone, TTS voice, live captions
- 🏠 **The Lease Signing** — a real 18-clause lease with three poisoned clauses, plus a **plain-English jargon translator**
- 🚗 **The Toll Text** — a smishing scam generated by our own Scam Factory pipeline from a real FTC alert

During a round you can **flag any message with one of ten named manipulation tactics** (urgency pressure, fee burial, authority impersonation, isolation…). When you decide — take the deal, negotiate, or walk — an independent AI referee grades the whole transcript, and you get the **Evidence File**: your conversation annotated in the margins (CAUGHT / RESISTED / FELL FOR IT), with every buried mechanic literally *un-redacting* on screen, and one number in red: exactly what your decision would have cost.

The app even demos itself: a **hands-free auto-demo** takes the cursor, plays a real round, flags a tactic, takes the bad deal, and opens the evidence — nothing mocked, nothing pre-recorded.

## How we built it

Three strictly separated engines, because separation *is* the technical argument:

**1. The adversary engine.** Every scammer is a persona plus a JSON playbook — beats, escalation rules, and *required concessions* (accurate facts the character must admit when cornered precisely). Online, it's a live Claude character streamed over SSE with a hidden `[[beat:bN]]` marker tracking playbook progress. Offline, a scripted state machine runs the *same playbook* through the *same wire format* — kill the API key and the demo cannot break.

**2. The referee.** A second, independent model call receives the transcript, our 10-tactic taxonomy, the playbook's ground truth, and the player's live flags, and returns strict-JSON findings. **The scammer never grades its own exam.** (Offline, a deterministic grader scores the round instead.)

**3. The damage engine.** Every dollar is deterministic TypeScript — pure functions, 27 unit tests, zero AI. The payday engine implements the real fee structure:

$$\text{APR} = \frac{\$15}{\$100} \times \frac{365}{14} \times 100\% \approx 391.07\%$$

The internship engine implements the Reg CC provisional-credit timeline: deposit a counterfeit \$2,480 check, "keep" \$350, forward \$2,130 — then day 7 the check bounces, the bank reverses the whole provisional credit, and the loss is

$$\$2{,}130 + \$35_{\text{returned-item fee}} = \$2{,}165 \;\approx\; 6.2 \text{ weeks of the promised salary}$$

Each scenario's key computation is also **independently re-run by Wolfram\|Alpha** at debrief time and badged on the Evidence File — two systems, one number, or the badge refuses to render.

**Stack:** React 18 + TypeScript + Vite, Tailwind on a custom case-file design system, Framer Motion (redaction reveals, stamp animations), Express + SQLite/Drizzle (including a hand-rolled 40-line session store so logins survive restarts), Anthropic API with SSE streaming, browser SpeechSynthesis/SpeechRecognition for voice mode, deployed on Render.

**Scaling content, not code:** our Scam Factory turns a pasted FTC alert into a complete new adversary — persona, five-tactic playbook, offline fallback, damage spec — with our code validating every field and freezing the math. The Toll Text scenario didn't exist until the pipeline built it.

## Challenges we ran into

- **Streaming around a hidden control channel.** The adversary streams tokens live, but its trailing `[[beat:bN]]` marker must never reach the screen — so the SSE writer holds back a 14-character tail until it's provably not the marker, then strips it before persisting.
- **Making "offline" identical, not degraded.** The scripted fallback had to match the live path's wire shape exactly — same SSE frames, same referee schema — so the UI can't tell which engine it's talking to.
- **A demo that drives a real app.** The auto-demo types into actual React controlled inputs (native-setter events), clicks real buttons, and survives its own timing: we hit a bug where the progress bar re-rendered the popup 60×/second, and each render's fresh callback identity restarted the typewriter — permanently blank text — fixed by pinning callbacks in refs.
- **Making instant AI feel human.** Scripted replies arrived instantly, which read as robotic — we added a 1.1–1.9s "typing…" beat before the first token.
- **Small production landmines:** the popular SQLite session-store package hardcodes a `sessions` table name that collided with our rounds table (we wrote our own), and Vite binds IPv6 so our port-cleanup script had to scan both stacks.

## What we learned

That **trust is an architecture decision**: the moment we separated who plays the scammer, who grades, and who computes the money, every claim in our pitch became checkable. That accessibility is load-bearing when your whole thesis is "jargon is where the money hides" — the plain-English lease translator and the Atkinson Hyperlegible toggle aren't add-ons, they're the product. And that the most persuasive thing a hackathon project can do is lose to you convincingly: watching yourself get taken for \$2,165 by a chirpy recruiter teaches more than any infographic we could have made.

## What's next

Difficulty tiers where adversaries get subtler as your catch-rate rises, white-label playbook tooling so a credit union's fraud team can author scenarios without touching code, and a monthly adversary drop tracked to FTC alerts — because the scams don't stop evolving, and now neither do we.
